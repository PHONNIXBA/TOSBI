§0Scope

This document specifies the MVP protection product: a loss-linked, capped protection contract attached to a PerpDEX position, priced off-chain from a volatility table, reserved and settled on-chain, and fully backed by PerpDEX-supplied Protection Capital.

It covers product terms, pricing formulas, capital rules, settlement rules, anti-abuse rules, Soroban contract architecture, off-chain services, initial risk parameters, a worked example, monitoring, a test plan, and references.

Out of scope for v0.1: streaming/perpetual protection (Architecture 3), tranched vaults (Architecture 4), delta-hedging (Architecture 5).

§2Product terms

2.1 Trader chooses

  • A Protected Amount, A_min ≤ A ≤ min(A_max, position notional)
  • D Duration from the bucket menu {15m, 1h, 4h, 12h, 1d, 3d, 7d}. Off-menu durations permitted in v0.2 via interpolation (§3.4); v0.1 restricts to the menu.
  • R Coverage Range, R_min ≤ R ≤ R_max (initial 0.05–0.50)

2.2 TOSBI determines

P_start   = mark price from Position Contract at quote time
P_end     = P_start × (1 − R)          if LONG
          = P_start × (1 + R)          if SHORT
MaxPayout = Q × |P_start − P_end| = A × R
Premium   = Π        (§3)
Fee       = F        (§3.7)
Reserved  = MaxPayout

2.3 Payout at settlement

LONG:   Payout = min( Q × max(0, P_start − P_settle),  MaxPayout )
SHORT:  Payout = min( Q × max(0, P_settle − P_start),  MaxPayout )

equivalently  Payout = A × min( R, max(0, loss_fraction) )
              LONG:   loss_fraction = (P_start − P_settle) / P_start
              SHORT:  loss_fraction = (P_settle − P_start) / P_start

This is a bull put spread (LONG protection) or bear call spread (SHORT protection), held long by the trader.

§3Pricing

3.1 Black–Scholes building blocks

With risk-free rate r (set r = 0 for MVP; USDC carry is negligible at these tenors) and S = P_start:

d1(K) = [ ln(S/K) + (r + σ²/2) τ ] / (σ √τ)
d2(K) = d1(K) − σ √τ

Put(K)  = K e^(−rτ) N(−d2) − S N(−d1)
Call(K) = S N(d1) − K e^(−rτ) N(d2)

Reference: Black & Scholes (1973); Hull, Options, Futures and Other Derivatives, ch. 15.

3.2 Fair premium (per contract)

LONG:   Π_fair = Q × [ Put(P_start)  − Put(P_end)  ]      σ = σ_down(asset, τ)
SHORT:  Π_fair = Q × [ Call(P_start) − Call(P_end) ]      σ = σ_up(asset, τ)

sanity check (ATM, r = 0):   Put_ATM = Call_ATM ≈ 0.3989 × S × σ√τ

The two directions use different volatilities because crypto returns are asymmetric: falls are sharper and fatter-tailed than rises. With a single σ an ATM put spread and an ATM call spread price identically under Black–Scholes (r = 0), which would misprice one side.

Put(P_start) dominates because it is at-the-money at purchase; Put(P_end) is small for short tenors but must be computed — it matters for 3–7 day covers and small R.

3.3 Volatility table

σ_dir(asset, τ_bucket) is maintained off-chain, one value per (asset, bucket, direction): a σ_down calibrated on falls and a σ_up calibrated on rises.

Do not derive short-bucket σ by scaling annualized vol with √τ. Short-horizon crypto returns are fat-tailed; a √τ-scaled diffusion under-prices 15-minute to 4-hour covers (Matic, Packham & Härdle 2023; Du 2025).

Calibrate each bucket empirically. For bucket τ_b, collect rolling τ_b-horizon log returns over trailing window H_b (initial: 30 days for ≤ 12h buckets, 90 days for ≥ 1d). The quantile-matched σ is the σ at which the Black–Scholes spread price equals the empirical expected payoff:

down_τ = (P_0 − P_τ) / P_0 = 1 − exp(ret_τ)        loss fraction for a LONG over horizon τ_b
up_τ   = (P_τ − P_0) / P_0 = exp(ret_τ) − 1        loss fraction for a SHORT over horizon τ_b

σ_down,b = argmin_σ | [Put_BS(S,σ,τ_b)  − Put_BS(S(1−R_ref),σ,τ_b) ] / S  −  E_emp[ max(0, min(R_ref, down_τ)) ] |
σ_up,b   = argmin_σ | [Call_BS(S,σ,τ_b) − Call_BS(S(1+R_ref),σ,τ_b)] / S  −  E_emp[ max(0, min(R_ref, up_τ)) ] |

both sides as a fraction of A.   R_ref = 0.25 (or solve at 2–3 reference ranges and take the max)
floor:  σ_dir,b ≥ σ_floor(asset)     initial XLM 60%, BTC/ETH 50%, same floor both directions
cap:    σ_dir,b ≤ 300%
expect σ_down > σ_up for most assets in most regimes; do not force them equal

This absorbs jump risk into σ without a separate jump model. Refresh every 4 hours for buckets ≤ 12h, daily for ≥ 1d; re-price immediately on a vol-shock trigger (realized 1h vol > 2× table σ_1h).

v1.1 upgrade: SVI surface

k = ln(K/S)
w(k) = a + b [ ρ (k − m) + sqrt( (k − m)² + s² ) ],     σ²τ = w(k)

Subject to Gatheral–Jacquier no-arbitrage constraints; calibrated to Deribit/Bybit BTC/ETH surfaces and to empirical skew for XLM. Gives correct Put(P_end) at large R.

3.4 Off-bucket durations (v0.2)

Interpolate in total variance, not in σ:

w_dir,b = σ_dir,b² τ_b          (interpolate each direction separately)
w(τ)  = w_lo + (w_hi − w_lo) × (τ − τ_lo) / (τ_hi − τ_lo)      τ_lo ≤ τ ≤ τ_hi
σ(τ)  = sqrt( w(τ) / τ )

require w(τ) non-decreasing in τ (calendar no-arbitrage); if violated, bump w_hi

Long-term path: map D → q and price with the Amortizing Perpetual Option closed form (Feinstein 2025), which prices all durations from one formula. Note the provisional US patent on AmPOs before adopting.

The concentration hard cap (§4.2) binds before M_c gets large; that is intentional. Rationale: option market makers charge more as net inventory imbalance grows (Alexander et al. 2021); actuarially these are variance / standard-deviation premium principles (Kaas et al., ch. 5).

3.6 Minimum premium

Π = max( Π, Π_min ),      Π_min = max( 0.5 USDC, 0.10% × A )

3.7 Protection Fee

F = max( F_min, f_bps × A ),     initial F_min = 0.5 USDC, f_bps = 30 bp

Fee goes to TOSBI treasury; revenue share with the PerpDEX is a config parameter share_pdx ∈ [0,1], initial 0.

3.8 Total cost to trader

Cost = Π + F
Π → ProtectionVault (belongs to capital providers)
F → TOSBI treasury

§4Capital rules

4.1 Full backing (invariant, enforced on-chain)

Σ_{active} MaxPayout_i  ≤  K_available
K_available = vault_balance − pending_payouts − withdrawal_locked

Mint fails if the invariant would be violated.

4.2 Hard caps (enforced on-chain)

CapInitial value
Per (asset, direction) concentration C_{a,d}≤ 25% of K
Per asset, both directions≤ 40% of K
Per trader wallet, sum of active MaxPayout≤ 2% of K and ≤ 25,000 USDC
Per contract A_max / A_min50,000 / 20 USDC
Global utilization U≤ 90% (last 10% is buffer for oracle/settlement slippage)

4.3 Liquidity-relative cap (pricer-enforced, on-chain param)

Σ protected notional(a) ≤ κ × Depth_a       initial κ = 0.5

Depth_a = USDC needed to move asset a by R_ref across the venues the oracle reads, estimated daily. A manipulator must spend more than they can extract (Mackinga, Nadahalli & Wattenhofer 2022).

4.4 PerpDEX capital withdrawal

Withdrawals are queued: the requested amount becomes withdrawal_locked immediately (blocks new mints against it) and is releasable after T_withdraw (initial 7 days) provided the full-backing invariant still holds.

4.5 Premium accrual

Premiums enter the vault immediately and count toward K. Track premium_earned and payout_paid per epoch for loss-ratio monitoring (§11).

§5Settlement

5.1 Normal settlement (expiry)

P_settle = TWAP_oracle( asset, window = W(D), ending at T_expire )

W(D) = clamp( 300 s,  0.53 × sqrt(D_minutes) minutes,  1800 s )
size override:  if MaxPayout > 10,000 USDC then W = 1800 s
DurationW(D)DurationW(D)
15m5 min (floor)12h~14 min
1h5 min (floor)1d~20 min
4h~8 min3d, 7d30 min (cap)

Rationale: manipulation cost should scale with contract value. The 30-minute cap matches Bybit Perp Protect and Deribit convention. Settlement may be triggered by anyone at t ≥ T_expire; if the keeper is late by more than one oracle resolution, the contract uses the closest available window and emits LateSettlement.

5.2 Early settlement (position closed)

P_ref = lastprice_oracle(asset) at t_close (or nearest record)
if | P_close − P_ref | / P_ref ≤ δ_close:   P_settle = P_close      δ_close = 1.5%
else:                                       P_settle = P_ref        (do not trust an outlier close)

Prevents wash-trading a bad close price on a thin book. Partial close: v0.1 settles the whole contract on any close; v0.2 continues on Q' = min(Q, remaining size) with pro-rata MaxPayout and reserve release.

5.3 Liquidation of the underlying position

Treated as early settlement with P_close = liquidation price from the Position Contract, subject to the same δ_close check. Protection survives liquidation (matches Bybit).

5.4 Oracle safety checks (on-chain)

staleness:      reject if oracle.last_timestamp < now − 2 × oracle.resolution; retry later
deviation:      if secondary oracle present and |P_primary − P_secondary| / P_secondary > δ_oracle (2%):
                    P_settle = the value closer to the pre-window 1h TWAP
per-record cap: ignore any single record deviating > 15% from the prior record  (truncated-oracle pattern)
stale-max:      if stale > T_stale_max (2h): settle on last valid TWAP, emit DegradedSettlement, admin may pause mints

7.3 Contract record (persistent storage)

struct Protection {
    id: u64,
    trader: Address,
    perpdex: Address,
    position_id: BytesN<32>,
    asset: Symbol,
    direction: Direction,        // Long | Short
    q: i128,                     // units, 7 decimals
    p_start: i128,               // oracle decimals
    p_end: i128,
    max_payout: i128,            // USDC 7 decimals
    premium_paid: i128,
    fee_paid: i128,
    t_mint: u64,
    t_active: u64,               // t_mint + activation delay
    t_expire: u64,
    window_secs: u32,
    status: Status,              // Pending | Active | Settled | Cancelled
    settle_price: Option<i128>,
    payout: Option<i128>,
}

7.4 Storage and TTL

  • Protection records, vault balances, reserve totals, per-(asset,direction) totals, per-wallet totals: persistent storage.
  • Nonce set: persistent, pruned after settlement. Quote payloads: not stored.
  • Extend TTL on every write and on every read in settle; keeper extends TTL for all active records every 24h. Target TTL ≥ D + 14 days.
  • Never use temporary storage for anything that affects money.

7.5 Keeper

Off-chain service that (a) calls settle(id) at t_expire within one oracle resolution; (b) watches PerpDEX close events and calls settle_early(id); (c) extends TTLs; (d) posts vol-table hashes. Anyone may call settle; the keeper is a liveness convenience, not a trust dependency.

7.6 Required PerpDEX Position Contract interface

fn get_position(position_id: BytesN<32>) -> Position {
    owner: Address, asset: Symbol, direction: Direction,
    size: i128, entry_price: i128, mark_price: i128,
    status: PositionStatus,          // Open | Closed | Liquidated
    close_price: Option<i128>, close_ts: Option<u64>,
}
event PositionClosed(position_id, close_price, close_ts, reason)

If a PerpDEX cannot expose close_price on-chain, the adapter falls back to oracle-only early settlement (P_settle = P_ref).

7.7 Oracle adapter (SEP-40)

Uses lastprice(asset), twap(asset, records), decimals(), resolution(). records = ceil(W / resolution). Read decimals() at runtime; never hard-code. Primary: Reflector. Secondary (deviation check only): Pyth or RedStone on Stellar.

If Reflector's public resolution is 5 minutes, the 5-minute floor equals one record. For ≤ 1h buckets either use Reflector's paid faster feed or accept single-record settlement with the per-record cap.

7.8 Events

Minted, Activated, Settled{price, payout}, SettledEarly, LateSettlement, DegradedSettlement, CapitalDeposited, WithdrawalRequested, WithdrawalExecuted, ParamsUpdated, Paused.

7.9 Admin and upgrades

Multisig admin (TOSBI + PerpDEX signers). Parameter changes time-locked 24h except pause. Contract upgrade via Soroban WASM upgrade behind the same time-lock.

§10Worked example

§11Monitoring

LR_epoch = Σ payouts / Σ premiums       epoch = 7 days; also 30-day rolling

target:   LR ≤ 0.80 rolling 30d
raise ρ or bucket σ:   LR > 1.0 for two consecutive weeks
lower ρ / loadings:    LR < 0.4 for 8 weeks with low utilization

Track LR per bucket and per asset to see which tenors are mispriced; expect short buckets to fail first. Also: utilization time series; max concentration; oracle staleness incidents; settlement latency; count of δ_close overrides (wash-trading signal); quote-to-mint conversion by price level (demand elasticity).

§12Test and validation plan

  1. Pricing backtestXLM/BTC/ETH hourly, 2022–2026. Simulate selling every bucket × R daily; compute realized LR under the empirical-σ calibration; confirm LR < 1 over the full period; inspect worst 30-day windows (May 2022, Nov 2022, Aug 2024, Oct 2025, Feb 2026).
  2. Crash stressInstantaneous −30% and −50% with a fully concentrated book at cap; verify solvency and that payouts equal Σ MaxPayout.
  3. Oracle attack simulationInject single-record spikes of +15% / −15% / −40% into the TWAP window; confirm per-record cap and deviation logic bound payout error to < δ_oracle.
  4. Wash-close testClose position at ±3% from oracle; verify δ_close fallback.
  5. Invariant fuzzingRandomized mint/settle/withdraw sequences; assert full-backing and cap invariants after every step.
  6. TTL test7-day contract with no interaction; verify keeper extension and archival-restore path.
  7. LatencyKeeper settles within one oracle resolution of expiry ≥ 99% of the time on testnet.
  8. AuditApply to the Soroban Audit Bank via SCF Build.
